The old road climbs out of the valley through birches, wide enough for a cart and empty of them. Lamp-posts of black wood stand along it at every hundred paces, each with a stone cup at the top for oil. Every cup is dry. Behind you the hamlet's lights go out, one by one. Ahead the hill is a blacker shape against the dark, and there is a smell of wet linen on the air.

The bell tolls, and the birches shiver as if it had touched them.

::: gm
The road is a **journey**: the second thing this adventure teaches, after the vow. When the hero sets out, have them *Undertake a Journey* (+wits) and start a track: **`solo track add "The barrow road" --kind journey --rank troublesome`** (it's short). Each waypoint is a roll; on a hit they reach one and mark progress (three boxes each at this rank); on a miss the road turns on them and they Pay the Price. Three or four waypoints will do it. Give each one a sign from the road (`solo table road_signs`) and let the hero look at it; ask the oracle what it means.

When the journey comes to its end, *Reach Your Destination*: a progress roll against the track. On a strong hit the hero arrives with the advantage; on a weak hit there is a complication at the door; on a miss the road leads them wrong and they have to walk it again. When they arrive, commit the fact **`road.done`** so the way to the mouth opens. (They can always go back to Marrow Ford; the exit is open.)

**Nothing on this road will hurt the hero on its own.** The bell is patient. What it does is make them afraid: misses tick the bell clock, and the omens it rolls are the road's mood, not its teeth. If the clock has passed 4, the road is not empty: the sleepers of the hamlet are on it now, walking uphill in the dark. The hero may turn one back (a strong hit) but can't turn back all of them.

**Moves that fit here:** Undertake a Journey, Reach Your Destination, Face Danger (a slope, a ford, a fright), Gather Information (the signs), Make Camp (if the hero wants to stop, and Supply to do it), Secure an Advantage (light a lamp from the hero's own oil; the first of the road's posts to be lit will change how the dark feels).
