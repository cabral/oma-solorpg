The mill is silent, its wheel stopped in the shallows. The door hangs open on a room where a boy's blanket lies thrown back, and a woman stands at the empty bed with her arms folded tight, not crying. Small wet footprints cross the floor, toward the door and the river. They are bare. They are not running.

"He didn't take his shoes," she says. "He took the heel of the loaf, off the table. That is all."

**Brenna** is Wen's mother, a miller's widow, and she is holding herself together with both hands. Play her flat and practical: she's decided she will not fall apart until her boy is home. She is the best person in the hamlet to bond with, and the first who'll give the hero a reason that isn't duty.

**What she knows.** The footprints go out of the door, across the ford and up the hill road; Wen's eyes were open and he didn't answer his name. She remembers her aunt Marta's rhyme in pieces: *"three lamps, three names, oil and bread."* She never knew what it was for. (If the hero already has the rhyme from Osk, she can supply what Osk forgot: the *bread for the ground* is a real loaf, broken and left at the door.) She has a jar of lamp-oil in the store-room and will give it to anyone who will bring Wen back.

**A bond:** Brenna will swear herself to a hero who promises her something they can keep. Forge a Bond (+heart); on a strong hit, mark a tick on their bonds; ask the oracle if unsure what she asks in return.

**The clue in the room:** the heel of the loaf was *bread for the ground*: Wen was carrying the offering in his sleep. Something under the hill has been asking for the old rite, in the only way it knows.

**Moves that fit here:** Gather Information (the footprints, the loaf), Forge a Bond, Compel, Sojourn (a night's rest in a house that has a fire).
