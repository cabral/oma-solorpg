The eighth stroke is not a bell. It is a door, somewhere under the hill, opening at last on the whole cold length of the ford's dead. They come down the hill road the way the sleepers went, barefoot, in linen, and they do not hurry. There is no need. They know the way to every door in Marrow Ford, and they have been waiting a very long time for someone to answer.

::: gm
This is the story failing, and it should feel like one: not an ambush, but a debt coming due. The dead are not here to hurt the living so much as to *take back* what the ford owes them. The ringers, the elder, the hero: everyone who was ever part of the oath.

The hero can still act. They are at Marrow Ford (or wherever they were, if they were far away, with the bell too loud to think). There is one chance left: they can go up the hill against the tide of the dead and answer at the bell, and Hild will hear them. *Face Danger* (+heart) with everything they've got; on a strong hit, she listens, and the dead stop. On a weak hit, the price is high: the hero, or someone they love. On a miss, the ford is lost and this is where the story ends. Write it as a chronicle entry and a stop: let it be sad, and let it be brief.
